# Origami Booklet Traditional Edition — step transcriptions

Source: Ikeda Shoten, ISBN 978-4-262-15250-9, read from the owner's photos (kept outside the repo).
Steps are paraphrased in our own words — no text or images copied. Step numbers match the book's.
Companion research (book identification, engine gaps): `2026-10-08-origami-booklet-research.md`.
Plan: one PR per model; engine gaps and bases land first in their own PRs.

**Excluded (owner decision 2026-10-08): models needing more than one sheet.** Shuriken (#4, two sheets). Hakama stays as a single-sheet model; gluing it onto a Yakko-san is optional and out of scope. Cut models stay in: the engine will gain cuts. 23 models; book numbering is kept.

## Engine: cuts (owner decision 2026-10-08)

Every cut in the book is a slit — no piece is ever cut off. Obake: a short slit, cut part pulled out.
Kiji: a diagonal cut halfway into the tail, splitting it. Swallow: a 5 cm tail split, then a 1 cm slit the other
tail threads through (that threading is a lock, same gap as Camellia's tuck). Snail: a slit in the tip,
spread into feelers.

Build slits only: a distinct line traces along the cut path, then the paper parts so each side folds on its
own. Cutting a piece off (with the smaller piece dissolving) is deferred until a model needs it.

## Bases (library entries)

The book defines these shapes once (pp.15–16, 65) and later models start from them. Each becomes its own
library entry, and models that start from one say so ("Builds on: …").

| Base | Book page | How it's made | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square base | p15 | Valley diagonals, mountain midlines, collapse ★ to ★ and ☆ to ☆ | Sanbo (after a blintz), Basic Shape 1, Basic Shape 2; Snail makes one by another route |
| Kite base | p16 | Crease a diagonal, fold the two edges at one corner to it | Kiji, Whale |
| Kannon fold (cupboard) | p16 | Crease in half, fold two opposite edges to the centre | Chochin |
| Double kannon fold | p16 | Kannon fold in both directions (top/bottom edges to centre) | Pig |
| Blintz base | p16 | Pinch the centre, fold all four corners to it | Sanbo, Yakko-san, Sumo, Ferryboat |
| Basic Shape 1 (squashed square base) | p65 | Square base, open end up, squash all four flaps | Ayame, Hana-tato, Obake (→ Hawk) |
| Basic Shape 2 (bird base) | p65 | Square base, open end down, petal-fold front and back | Okago, Swallow (and our existing Crane) |

Models that continue from another model's step (a "builds on" link to a model, not a base):
- Hakama ← Yakko-san step 5 (triple blintz)
- Piano ← Fox mask step 5
- Hawk ← Obake step 4

No base: Dove, Camellia, Fox mask, Iwai-zutsumi, Cicada, Hat.

Basic Shape 1 is squash-only (no petal fold) — confirmed on p65 photo.

## 1. Sanbo, footed offering stand (Ashitsuki Sanbō) — IMG_0067–0068, pp.66–67

**Builds on:** Blintz base → Square base

- **Paper:** one square. Start white side up, so the blintz flaps show the coloured side.
- **Starting base:** blintz base, then collapsed into a square base.

Steps:

1. Make a blintz base by folding all four corners to the centre. Crease the blintzed square (it is now half the area) on both its diagonals and both midlines, then collapse it into a square base with the coloured side out.
2. The square base sits as a diamond with its open end down. The blintz flaps are now hidden inside the top layer as a left and a right triangle. Open those two triangles outward and up, and flatten them so the upper half of the front becomes a rectangular pocket. The lower half stays a triangle with the centre slit.
3. This is the same move in progress: the top opens out into a box-shaped pocket.
4. The result is a rectangle across the upper half with a triangle below it. Repeat steps 2–3 on the back.
5. Work on the front rectangle:
   - Add two short 45° mountain creases at its top corners. Each runs from the top edge, about ¼ of the rectangle's width in from the corner, down and out to the side edge.
   - Add a horizontal valley crease across the rectangle at half its height.
   - Open the rectangular pocket and press the top edge down, as the arrow shows, so the upper half of the rectangle folds down inside along the half-height valley. The corner mountain creases let the corners collapse.
6. Same move in progress: open the pocket with your fingers and press it flat.
7. Repeat steps 5–6 on the back. The front now shows an open white box shape at the top with grey triangles at its sides. Then book-fold the upper flap from right to left along the vertical centre line, and do the same on the back.
8. A clean grey face is now showing, with the centre slit running down the middle. On each half, fold the top inner corner (the one beside the slit) outward and down. The valley runs at 45° from the top edge to the slit and forms a small right isosceles triangle. Repeat on the back.
9. Fold the outer edges of each half in to that half's own centre line, so each half is folded in half lengthwise and the edge lands about ¼ of the total width from the outside. Repeat on the back.
10. Fold the bottom up along a horizontal valley at mid-height. This bottom part becomes the legs. Repeat on the back.
11. Rotate the model 180° so the legs point down. Put your fingers into the centre and pull the two sides apart while pressing the middle down. This opens the top into a flat tray.
12. Shape it. The finished model is **3D**: a tray standing on four triangular legs.

- **Flags:** 3D open-out; also a pocket opening (steps 2 and 5).
- **Uncertain:**
  - Step 2: I can't tell for sure whether the dotted horizontal line across the middle of the diamond is a crease or a hidden edge. The exact squash geometry of the "rectangular pocket" isn't visible; only the result is drawn.
  - Step 5: the ¼ position of the corner creases is my estimate from the drawing.
  - Step 8: it's unclear which layers the corner triangle goes through (probably all the front layers of that half).

## 2. Yakko-san, servant figure (Yakko-san) — IMG_0069, p.68

**Builds on:** Blintz base

- **Paper:** one square, white side up.
- **Starting base:** blintz base.

Steps:

1. Make a blintz base: all four corners to the centre.
2. Turn the paper over and fold the four corners to the centre again. This is the second blintz.
3. The result is a square made of four small squares.
4. Turn over and fold the four corners to the centre once more. This is the third blintz.
5. The result shows four small diamonds, each with a triangular pocket. Turn over.
6. Put a finger into the bottom triangular pocket and open it. Push the two sides of the pocket outward, flattening along a horizontal mountain line through that unit's left and right corners. The bottom diamond becomes a rectangle hanging below the centre.
7. Repeat on the left and right pockets. The top pocket is left as a triangle, which becomes the head.
8. **Flat**: a figure with a pointed head on top, rectangular sleeves to the left and right, and a rectangular body below.

- **Flags:** none beyond the squash-opening of the pockets (insert finger and open).
- **Uncertain:** none major.

## 3. Hakama, trousers (Hakama) — IMG_0070, p.69

**Builds on:** Yakko-san (#2) step 5

- **Paper:** one square, white side up.
- **Starting base:** Yakko-san step 5 (the triple blintz).

Steps:

1. Start from Yakko-san step 5 and turn over.
2. Open the top triangular pocket and the bottom one, and squash each into a rectangle as in Yakko-san step 6. The left and right pockets stay as triangles.
3. Fold in half upward along the horizontal valley through the left and right corners (the centre line). The bottom rectangle swings up onto the top one.
4. Open the top slightly. Pull the hidden inner triangular flaps out of the left and right triangles, swinging them out and down.
5. While pulling a flap out, push at the arrow (the outer bottom corner) so it becomes an outside reverse fold. The flap turns into a leg angled down and out at about 45°. The right side is shown.
6. Repeat on the left side.
7. **Flat**: a waistband (two rectangles) on top and two angled legs.

- **Optional:** slide Yakko-san's body between the hakama's flaps and glue them together.
- **Flags:** outside reverse fold; optional multi-sheet with glue (the Yakko-san plus the hakama).
- **Uncertain:** step 4. It's unclear exactly which layer is pulled out; the drawing only shows a curved hidden-edge line inside each triangle.

## 5. Dove (Hato) — IMG_0072, p.71

**Builds on:** —

- **Paper:** one square, white side up, laid as a diamond.

Steps:

1. Crease the horizontal diagonal, then unfold. Fold in half along the vertical diagonal so the right corner meets the left corner. The result is a triangle with its vertical fold edge on the right and its apex pointing left.
2. Fold both layers together along a vertical valley about ⅓ of the triangle's width in from the right fold edge. The apex swings over to the right and sticks out past the edge.
3. Take only the top layer's point (now on the right) and fold it back to the left along a vertical valley inside the strip.
4. A white diamond now sits across the middle of a vertical strip. Fold the top half down onto the bottom half along the horizontal line through the diamond's left and right corners. The result is a T-shape: a horizontal bar with points at both ends on top, and a strip hanging down.
5. Fold the hanging strip (a wing) up along a valley that crosses just below the top bar and rises slightly to the right. Repeat on the back. Both wings now point up.
6. Make the head by inside-reverse-folding the left point of the horizontal body downward.
7. **Flat** (lightly 3D if the wings are spread): a bird with its wings raised.

- **Flags:** inside reverse fold.
- **Uncertain:**
  - Step 2: "about ⅓" is the book's own approximation, and my measurement gives roughly 0.3 from the fold edge.
  - Step 3: where exactly the back-fold line sits (and so how big the white diamond is) isn't marked.
  - Step 5: the angle of the wing crease is only roughly drawn.

## 6. Camellia (Tsubaki) — IMG_0073–0074, pp.72–73

**Builds on:** —

- **Paper:** one square, white side up.
- **Overview:** this is a cyclic twist. Each of the four edges in turn is folded to a diagonal along a 22.5° line through a corner, and the last flap is woven under the first.

Steps:

1. Crease both diagonals. Then fold the top-left corner over until it lands on the bottom-left-to-top-right diagonal, folding through the bottom-left corner. This lays the left edge along that diagonal, like a kite fold. The crease runs from the bottom-left corner to the top edge about 0.414 of the way across.
2. Fold the bottom edge up onto the top-left-to-bottom-right diagonal, folding through the bottom-right corner. The crease rises at 22.5° from the bottom-right corner to the left side. The bottom-left corner, together with the step-1 flap, swings up.
3. Add a vertical mountain crease at the left edge of the new flap. Open the gap between the layers (push in at the white arrow) and fold only the top layer toward the lower right, along a valley that runs diagonally up-right from the bottom of the mountain crease. This re-layers the step-1 and step-2 flaps so they overlap correctly.
4. Step 3 in progress.
5. Fold along the valley that runs from the top-right corner down to the bottom edge, near its right end. The right-hand point swings left, which brings the third edge in.
6. The model is rotated. Do the same move as in step 3: a horizontal mountain at the bottom (push in from below), plus a valley rising diagonally from its right end. Swing the flap up and to the right.
7. Fold along the dashed lines: a line across the upper-left grey region plus its continuation on the right. Swing the right flap down.
8. Repeat the step-3 move on the right: a vertical mountain at the right edge (push in at the arrow) and a diagonal valley from the top. Swing the flap to the upper left.
9. Step 8 in progress: lift the corner with your fingers.
10. Crease the inner flap's corner along a diagonal valley, then unfold.
11. Open up the parts folded in steps 7–8. Fold along a horizontal mountain and a diagonal valley from the left, bringing the flap down.
12. Collapse along the dashed and mountain lines so the last flap folds inward. This closes the cycle.
13. Tuck the corner down underneath the first flap. This is the cyclic lock.
14. In the white square at the centre, fold each of the four inner corners back a little, toward the outside.
15. **Flat** octagon. A grey pinwheel ring surrounds a white square centre with four small folded points.

- **Flags:** layers wrap in a cycle (pinwheel); pocket tuck/lock (step 13); flat.
- **Uncertain:**
  - Steps 3 and 6–13: the drawings are hard to follow, so the precise layer handling and crease positions are uncertain.
  - The 22.5° geometry for steps 1–2 is confirmed. Every later edge-to-diagonal fold probably repeats it, rotated 90°, but the book doesn't say so.
  - Steps 10–12 (reopening and collapsing the last flap so it goes under the first) are only roughly described here.
  - Step 14: the size of the inner corner folds isn't marked.
  - **This model will need a physical test fold.**

## 7. Iris (Ayame) — IMG_0075–0076, pp.74–75

**Builds on:** Basic Shape 1

- **Paper:** one square, white side up, so the colour is on the outside of the square base.
- **Starting base:** Basic Shape 1, the squashed square base (see IMG_0066).

Steps:

1. Start from Basic Shape 1. Book-fold the upper flap from right to left, and do the same on the back.
2. A smooth kite face is now showing: a short upper part and a long point at the bottom.
3. Fold the two upper edges (from the top point to the side corners) to the vertical centre line, then unfold.
4. Petal-fold the top layer downward. Use mountain folds along the step-3 creases and a horizontal valley joining the lower ends of those creases, at the height of the side corners. The top point swings down past the bottom.
5. Step 4 in progress: the sides fold in to the centre as the flap is pulled down.
6. The result shows a white triangle on top and a long grey petal pointing down. Repeat on the other three faces.
7. Fold the upper flap (the downward petal) back up along the horizontal at the widest point. Repeat on the other three faces.
8. The result is a long diamond with a cross-mark at the widest line.
9. Fold the lower edges of the top layer to the centre line. Each crease runs from the bottom point up to the widest line, which narrows the lower part.
10. Repeat on the other three faces.
11. Fold one upper petal down along a horizontal valley at the widest point.
12. Repeat on the other three petals.
13. Pull the folded-down petals outward and open the flower so the tips spread out.
14. Shape it. Roll each petal around a ballpoint pen to curl it.

- **Final:** **3D** iris: four petals curl outward and down, with narrow points standing up in the centre.
- **Flags:** petal fold; 3D open-out; curl/shape.
- **Uncertain:**
  - Between steps 6 and 7 the drawing shrinks and probably involves swinging layers to show a fresh face, but no explicit "swing flaps" step is drawn.
  - Step 13: the English caption says "bring up" while the arrows point outward/sideways. I read it as opening the petals outward.
  - The ends of the step-9 creases at the widest line are an estimate.

## 8. Palanquin (Okago, 御駕籠)

**Builds on:** Basic Shape 2 (bird base)
Photos: IMG_0077 (p76), IMG_0078 (p77)

- **Paper:** 1 square.
- **Starting base:** basic shape 2 (bird base), open corners pointing up.

**Steps**
1. Valley-fold the lower point of the front flap up along the horizontal line through the side corners, the widest line. Repeat behind.
2. Fold the top layer down along a horizontal line at about the level of the side corners. Repeat behind.
3. Swing the upper flap to the left like turning a page, hinged on the vertical center. Repeat behind.
4. Fold the lower edges of the top layer in to the vertical centerline. Repeat behind. The shape is now narrow, with a long point at the top.
5. Open the triangular pockets at the two sides and squash them out sideways, giving the shape of step 6. Repeat behind.
6. Bend the two sharp points toward you; a side view shows this half done. Rotate the model 180°.
7. The two points now stick out sideways as carrying poles. Fold the front body flap up along a line at pole level, just under the top. Turn over.
8. A small triangle now pokes up above the poles. Fold its tip down along a line about one third of the way down from the tip.
9. Open the center slit and tuck the folded tip inside.
10. Fold up along the line at pole level on this side too. Turn over.
11. Push your fingers into the center and puff out the bottom so the body becomes a 3D box. Fold the upper triangle down over the front.
12. Tuck the tip into the center pocket.
13. Shape it. Finished.

- **Result:** 3D palanquin with two poles.
- **Flags:** 3D open-out, inflate/puff, pocket tuck.
- **Uncertain:**
  - Exact height of the fold lines in steps 1, 2 and 8. The diagrams show approximate dotted lines, and I could not confirm any landmark beyond "about side-corner level" and "about 1/3 from the tip".
  - Whether step 5 is a squash or a plain open-out.

## 9. Flower Tato (Hana-tato, 花たとう), a folded paper case

**Builds on:** Basic Shape 1 (folded then unfolded)
Photo: IMG_0079 (p78)

- **Paper:** 1 square. It starts white side up after unfolding; the corner flaps show the colored side.
- **Starting base:** basic shape 1 (frog base), folded and then completely unfolded. This leaves the 22.5° radial creases and the octagon creases.

**Steps**
1. Fold basic shape 1, then unfold it to the flat square.
2. Fold the 4 corners in. Each crease joins the points where the 22.5° creases meet the edges, about 0.29 of the side from each corner. The corner tip lands at about (0.29, 0.29). The outline becomes a regular octagon.
3. The result is an octagon with colored corner triangles. Turn over and rotate.
4. Make small valley folds at each of the 8 octagon corners along the dashed lines shown.
5. Turn over. Valley-fold along a dashed crease of the inner octagon so that ★ (the midpoint of one outer edge) reaches ☆ (the center).
6. Fold so ● (a point on the edge just folded, near the center) meets ○. This uses an existing crease and makes a twist-collapse.
7. Step 6 in progress. Repeat steps 5 to 7 on the remaining 7 sides, working around the octagon.
8. Lift the first fold and tuck the last one underneath it to close the pinwheel.
9. Finished: a flat octagonal case with an 8-point pinwheel center.

- **Result:** flat. It opens and closes like a tato wrapper.
- **Flags:** pinwheel cycle, pocket tuck/lock (the last flap goes under the first).
- **Uncertain:**
  - The size of the step 4 corner folds.
  - The exact location of ● and ○ in step 6. The photo resolution did not let me pin them to a landmark.
  - Whether the step 2 fold position is defined by the 22.5° crease or simply by "make a regular octagon". It is geometrically the same either way.

## 17. Celebration Wrapper with Crane (Iwai-zutsumi)

**Builds on:** —
Photos: IMG_0087 (p86, steps 1–10), IMG_0088 (p87, steps 11–20; page number not visible). Arranged by Susumu Nakajima. The book's cross-reference is p49.

- **Paper:** 1 square, colored side up, placed as a diamond.
- **Base:** none.

**Steps**
1. Valley fold the vertical diagonal (top corner to bottom corner) and unfold. Pinch the horizontal diagonal at the center only.
2. Fold the left and right corners to the center point. The fold lines are vertical at 1/4 and 3/4 of the horizontal diagonal. Result: a tall hexagon d/2 wide, with two white triangles whose tips meet at the center.
3. Turn over.
4. Fold the long left and right edges to the vertical centerline (fold lines at ±d/8). Let the triangles underneath swing out to the front. Result: a strip d/4 wide, with a white diamond about d/2 wide lying across the middle. The diamond is the two former triangles, now overlapping.
5. Fold the diamond's right half over to the left along the vertical centerline. This gives a 2-layer triangle pointing left.
6. Fold the upper layer only along a line from the triangle's bottom corner upward. The flap's left tip swings right.
7. Fold that narrow flap in half again.
8. Fold the upper layer back to the right along the vertical centerline.
9. Fold the stacked layers right to left along the slanted dotted line.
10. Result. Repeat steps 6–9 on the left (lower) layer, mirrored.
11. Enlarged view: make small folds at the top shoulders of the two narrow centre flaps.
12. Valley fold the lower part upward along a horizontal line, roughly 2/3 of the way down.
13. Upper flap only: fold the two bottom corners and unfold, to precrease.
14. Open the middle layer.
15. Squash the bottom pocket flat into a diamond, using the step-13 creases.
16. Petal fold. Valley fold horizontally across the top of the diamond. The mountain creases from step 13 run up from the bottom tip. Lift the bottom point up so the sides fold in. This makes a tall, narrow white point.
17. Fold the narrow point in half along its vertical axis, right over left.
18. Inside reverse fold the tip to make the crane's head and beak.
19. Mountain fold the top point behind, along a horizontal line at the base of the top diamond.
20. Finished.
